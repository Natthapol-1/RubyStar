# Jupiter

[Overview](README.md)

`/challenge/jupiter` is a storm-and-pursuit fight against the Storm Reaper. Three HP phases share the charge/pursuit/race loop. Phase 2 adds Surge Cells and room reconfiguration; phase 3 adds mini storms and the Reaper's slowing trail. Tuning is in `JUPITER` in `constants.ts`, gameplay in `jupiter.ts` and `jupiter-cover.ts`, rendering in `jupiter-art.ts`.

Regular alien spawning is disabled in Jupiter. The Reaper and electrical map hazards remain active; existing aliens can still be damaged by storms and fences.

Jupiter’s book button beside Begin Challenge opens [The Man Who Never Stops](JUPITER-LORE.md), the tale of the Storm Reaper and the runners of Jupiter’s lightning relay.

## Guardian loop

- Three phases of 1700 / 2040 / 2040 HP (5780 total; phases 2 and 3 have 20% more HP than phase 1), shown as three guardian segments. Only the final defeat wins. Older equal-band run snapshots retain their current phase HP percentage when loaded with these values.
- Reaching the next HP phase immediately interrupts charging, pursuit or Shard Race and starts a fresh five-second charge for round one. Remaining shards, Surge Cells, overdrive, mini storms, boss dashes, discharge effects, hallway seals and pending room reconfiguration end. A phase-ending pickup resolves only once; leftover pickups cannot damage or heal him afterward. Player Volt Fences remain. The transition shows a PHASE banner and retains the new phase's energy capacity.
- **Charge:** he stands still for 5 seconds while energy fills to 100. He rises and tenses while his blades accelerate, four inward feeds and rising side arcs use the same rectangular plasma cuts and dark-teal/mint/white palette as Volt Fence and his melee lightning. Small capacitor pips fill along his shell; near-full charge adds short branching cuts. His face and animated blades remain clear. He takes 80% less damage.
- **Pursuit:** the map switches from Jupiter bronze/cream to mint-cyan storm colors. Your current chamber becomes the fixed safe room; if charging ends in a hallway, the nearest chamber is chosen. Other chambers have a 1.5-second storm grace period, then deal 14 damage to you and 7 to a placed Ruby every .5 seconds. Storm pulses also deal 14 damage to regular aliens in those rooms, respecting their defenses; the Reaper is immune. Storm kills give no player kill rewards. Hallways remain traversable. Teleporting does not move the safe room; bring Ruby or leave it safely.
- He hunts you only at 7.1 px/tick, about 5% faster than your unmodified 6.75 px/tick on-foot pursuit speed, with his existing 20-damage slash and 24-tick attack cooldown. When farther than eight tiles away, he gains a smooth distance-based catch-up multiplier, reaching 2.25× at twenty tiles. It fades back to normal inside eight tiles and stacks with his dash and fence slow. It applies only during pursuit. Your movement is 50% faster during pursuit. Carrying, normal speed skills and other modifiers still apply. He also keeps his standard hallway seal: when you share a hallway, its end caps close for 4 seconds, with an 8-second seal cooldown. The corridor remains fightable inside and reopens on expiry or his defeat.
- Energy loses 1.2 points per second (about 83 seconds for a full bar without hits or tile triggers). Each actual HP of damage he takes also removes .4 energy. The 80% damage reduction remains active throughout pursuit.
- His dash has the same 3-second cooldown as Volt Fence. When ready, he immediately gains a committed .4-second dash without pausing or winding up with a 6x initial speed that quickly eases back to normal. The bright frame, ground pips and afterimages show the active boost.
- Pursuit energy starts from a phase factor of 1 + .2 per phase after the first; phases 2 and 3 also receive a 1.155 capacity multiplier. Phase 1 uses 100/120 for the first/second pursuit, phase 2 uses 139/166, and phase 3 uses 162/194 after rounding. The second pursuit retains its 20% bonus before rounding. Charging fills to that pursuit's capacity; the HUD's ENERGY bar under his HP segments (the same labelled row layout as other maps' bars) scales each full bar to the same width.
- After the second pursuit, storms end and he becomes **exposed** until every shard is collected. Damage reduction is removed and he stops attacking. **Shard Race:** 32 golden shards appear on open floor, eight per room, spaced apart and away from the player, boss, Ruby and charged tiles. The player and Reaper race to collect them. Each player pickup calls down lightning on him for 1% of current phase maximum HP (17 in phase 1; 20.4 in phases 2–3) and heals you 4 HP (`shardHeal`); his pickups heal him for 1.5% of current phase maximum HP (25.5 in phase 1; 30.6 in phases 2–3), capped at his current phase's HP ceiling so defeated phases cannot return. Each shard has one collector and disappears immediately. Shards do not respawn. The race has no time limit; collecting the last shard restarts charging. Phase resets and encounter cleanup clear remaining shards.
- During Shard Race, the player’s unmodified on-foot base speed is 9 px/tick; the Reaper uses 8 px/tick. The normal speed skill and teleport-granted boost use a reduced 1.4× multiplier during Shard Race, instead of 2.2×. Their duration is unchanged. Carrying, Volt Fence dash, slow and other movement modifiers still apply. He moves at 8 px/tick toward the nearest reachable shard by walking distance, respecting cover and hallway seals. He retargets stolen shards, waits if none are reachable and does not chase the player or follow a fixed circuit. Volt Fence still reduces his speed to 18%, letting you steal shards. A dash can collect a shard crossed between frames; teleporting collects only at the destination. All collection and timers pause in menus.
- Large stepped golden minimap markers show available shards instead of the old escape route. Player pickups show a mint-white lightning strike on him and a golden pickup burst; his pickups show a mint healing transfer and a brief heal flash. The map returns to warm Jupiter colors and his armor remains drained slate-teal with amber core cells. Then the loop restarts.

## Later phases

From phase 2, rooms reconfigure during pursuit. Every 4–6 seconds of pursuit, roughly 60% of the substantial interior cover sections in each chamber are selected for new positions and orientations. Sections use bars, L shapes and rectangular clusters with the same tile area as the cover they replace. Single tiles, perimeter walls, hallway openings and pads stay fixed. The initial layout has 33 eligible sections, so a normal reshuffle changes about twenty sections across the station rather than a few single tiles.

New footprints appear as faint mint outlines for 1.2 seconds, then the old sections retract over .3 seconds and replacements rise over .3 seconds. Old collision stays until retraction completes; collision switches atomically when replacements begin rising. Paths refresh for the Reaper, other enemies and mini storms. All interior floor cells must remain connected to their chamber's pad, preserving access to entrances and Surge Cells. Placement avoids player movement, Ruby, enemies, mini storms, active fences, resources, bombs, charged tiles and pickups; pads and Surge Cells retain extra surrounding clearance. An actor or relocated pickup entering a marked footprint cancels that section. The combined layout is checked again before collision changes; an unsafe combination keeps the old layout.

Reconfiguration adds no damage and preserves cover area. Timers, preview and animation pause in menus and survive run saves. Charging and Shard Race hold the current layout steady and cancel any unfinished preview or animation safely; encounter cleanup does the same. Older sliding-cover saves release their departure reservation before using the new system. The rule-sheet card reuses the actual retraction, footprint and rising-cover painters. [Room reconfiguration](visual-reference/jupiter-moving-cover.png) · [Card animation](visual-reference/jupiter-guide-cover.png).

The Shard Race card animates player and Reaper pickups in a brisk 2.5-second loop: collected shards disappear, player pickups strike the Reaper with lightning, and boss pickups show the mint healing flash. The charged-floor card shows the player stepping onto a tile with a small blue contact flash and scattered sparks at their feet in a 1.6-second loop. Both keep the full impact-effect duration with short movement and pauses. Charged-floor triggers show a compact blue contact flash with a few pale sparks spreading from the player's feet. The ongoing slow retains its sparse blue suit current. Reaper dashes add long directional plasma trails, pale speed streaks and an advancing pressure frame. [Blue contact flash](visual-reference/jupiter-slow-impact.png) · [Reaper dash](visual-reference/jupiter-dash-effect.png).

- **Phase 2+:** two Surge Cells (`fragments` in code) stay on clear floor in the pursuit's safe room, away from hazards and bodies. They do not expire. A single overdrive meter fills over eight seconds during pursuit, then grants him 35% extra speed until a pickup. While full, Overdrive reduces Jupiter Storm and Volt Fence slow strength on him to one-fifth. It is a mint vertical bar beside his HUD health, shown only during pursuit and using the Sun rage-track style without visible text, numbers or percentages. Surge Cells have no countdown indicators. From 70% charge (`OVERDRIVE_WARNING_FROM` in `jupiter-art.ts`) a mint screen wash and edge glow build toward full, and pulse while overdrive is active; it shows only during phase 2+ pursuit. Collect either to reset this bar and boost, stop his current dash, and strike him for 1% of current phase HP (20.4) through pursuit armor. Each pickup immediately relocates that cell elsewhere in the same chamber, keeping two active. Charging, Shard Race and encounter cleanup remove the Surge Cells and reset overdrive.
- **Phase 3:** two mini storms per chamber roam along actual floor routes during pursuit. They persist into Shard Race, where storms within six tiles in your chamber chase you. Roaming speed is 2.8 px/tick; chasing speed is 4.6. Contact deals 10 damage and knocks you back two tiles, respecting walls and normal defenses. A shared one-second contact cooldown prevents overlapping storms from stacking hits. They do not harm Ruby or the boss. Charging and encounter cleanup clear them. All movement, charge windows and hit cooldowns pause in menus.
- **Phase 3 Reaper trail:** walking and dashing leave a floor-tile trail during pursuit and Shard Race. Each tile lasts 5 seconds, fading over its last .75 seconds; moving over an existing trail refreshes it, but standing still does not. At most 65 tiles remain; adding a tile at the cap replaces the oldest residue, while refreshing a tile does not consume another slot. Walls and teleport pads are excluded, and room reconfiguration removes trails covered by new walls. Contact reduces player movement to 70%, refreshed while on the trail and ending .2 seconds after leaving. The stronger charged-tile slow takes precedence rather than multiplying; crowd-control immunity prevents the trail slow. Trails deal no damage and add no Reaper energy. They persist into Shard Race, clear on charging or encounter cleanup, pause in menus and survive saved runs. The art joins adjacent tiles into one continuous translucent teal residue, with a softly lit outer edge, a deeper inner wash and sparse moving mint current packets. Interior tile borders are omitted; the floor grid remains visible. Contact reuses the sparse slowed-player electricity. [Trail and fade](visual-reference/jupiter-reaper-trail.png) · [Rule-card animation](visual-reference/jupiter-guide-trail.png).
- Fragments are split teal capacitor halves with a stepped mint/white plasma core, distinct from the golden star-shaped race shards. Minimap markers use the same split capacitor shape. Mini storms use three broad, slightly transparent flat teal layers, widest at the top and narrowest at the bottom. The three wind layers use distinct mint/teal/blue-teal palettes, stepped silhouettes and broad broken color ribbons that flow through each layer without bevels or edge highlights. Thin branching lightning spans the funnel horizontally and vertically; its connected path changes every three gameplay ticks, with intermittent forks and flashes instead of detached repeated particle chunks; compact minimap markers remain.

## Charged floor and chamber discharge

- Twenty charged electrical tiles are spread across the four rooms, five per room, on open floor away from you and Ruby. Stepping on an active tile bursts it, reducing your movement to 55% for 2 seconds. If he is pursuing, he gains 12 energy (capped at 100 in the first pursuit, 120 in the second). It deals no direct HP damage. After 3 seconds it relocates elsewhere in that room; a tile cannot repeatedly trigger while you stand on it.
- Charged tiles have a dark blue inset, pale electrical core and amber corner contacts, with blue minimap dots. Bursts show a local electrical shockwave and a STATIC · SLOWED status. The ongoing player slow shows only a few short blue/white electrical contacts at varying suit edges, with intermittent gaps, an occasional shoulder or boot discharge and a rare small spark. Positions are sampled on the simulation clock, so pausing freezes them. The visor stays clear, and it fades during the final .2 seconds. The charged-floor card reuses the same effect. [Slowed player](visual-reference/jupiter-static-slow.png).
- Each 5-second charge warns the whole room he is in with an electrical border, DISCHARGE countdown and minimap outline with a compact icon-only Discharge badge using the stepped energy-HUD lightning bolt. The Discharge countdown is drawn above battlefield tiles and slow effects. A hallway charge targets the nearest chamber.
- Completing charge releases a chamber-wide electrical explosion for 60 damage to you, a placed Ruby and regular aliens there. The Reaper and other rooms are unaffected. Normal defenses apply, including Volt Fence's brief 90% guard. Environmental kills grant no player kill rewards. The existing thunder recording accompanies pursuit entry; no synthesized charge/pursuit sound is added.

## Special skill (Middle Click): Volt Fence

- 3-second cooldown. Leaves an electric fence through your current tile, perpendicular to your facing direction, three tiles wide. Each side checks cover independently: three tiles when open, two beside one wall, one when both adjacent sides are blocked. The rendered endpoints and cast packets match collision, including fast crossings; horizontal fences preserve left/right clipping rather than mirroring it. [Clipped endpoints](visual-reference/jupiter-fence-clipping.png).
- Fences last **30 seconds**, fading during the last second. Each breaks on the first enemy crossing, dealing 18 damage before armor, slowing movement for 2 seconds and healing you 17 HP once, capped at your maximum. Ruby is not healed. Hitting him with a fence during pursuit removes an extra 12 energy, in addition to the energy lost from HP damage. An enemy crossing between simulation frames is still caught. Fences do not block you, Ruby, bullets or pathfinding.
- Normal slow: enemies retain 55% movement speed. During the Shard Race, the Reaper retains only **18%** speed, making saved fences useful traps. He is still slowed for the full 2 seconds.
- Casting also grants **90% damage reduction to you and Ruby for .6 seconds**, including meteors and storm damage. It applies before absorb shields, stacks with existing defenses and protects Ruby across rooms. A bright mint square guard shows the active window. Time it just before impact.
- Casting grants an independent .4-second forward dash with a 4x initial speed that quickly eases back to normal without spending the normal speed skill. It moves in your facing direction even without held movement, stops at walls, and stacks with the pursuit movement bonus. Your suit turns teal-green with electric afterimages during the dash, then returns to its normal colors. `skill-ready.wav` plays once when C recharges; successful casts play the supplied fence-cast recording at .85. The first half-second adds a cosmetic ignition: four capacitor contacts close in, an interrupted square pressure front opens, pale current packets travel to the existing emitters and short electrical wings flare beside the dash lane. The cast uses solid mint/teal rectangles and small pale cores with selective glow. A small eight-tick screen shake punctuates the release. Every floor tile actually crossed by the dash keeps a mint electrical imprint, inset rim and directional surface glint for about .6 seconds after departure, fading on the simulation clock. Their texture uses separated straight luminous conductor strips, without stair-shaped bolts ([tile stages](visual-reference/jupiter-volt-dash-tiles.png)). These footprints are cosmetic and respect cover. The persistent fence, endpoints and dash timing stay unchanged.
- Fences persist across charge/pursuit/escape transitions. Victory, No Boss and restart clear them.

## Celestial skill (press F): Jupiter Storm

The seven-tile lightning blast deals 20% of the current phase (340 in phase 1; 408 in phases 2–3) to him before protection, with the 9-second challenge recharge. Charge/pursuit protection reduces that to 68 / 81.6 and removes 27.2 / 32.64 energy during pursuit. Save it for the Shard Race to deal the full phase-scaled damage. All enemies hit move at 50% speed for one second on every map, including the regular modes. Recasts refresh the duration. Fence slow and Storm slow use whichever is stronger; they do not multiply. Ordinary alien damage retains the standard skill scaling. Meteors deal 10% of one phase before protection.

A meteor always hits him for 10% of the current phase (170 in phase 1; 204 in phases 2–3, `JUPITER.meteorShare`), ignoring the charge/pursuit protection. Its energy drain stays as if it were cut by that protection (13.6 / 16.32 energy during pursuit).

## Visuals, sound and controls

Jupiter's drifting banded atmosphere with white ovals, a starfield and small moon, broad dark storm-cloud banks, in-cloud flashes, occasional viewport-height forked lightning, distant lightning-rod towers, a large layered Jovian storm eye and a stepped Great Red Spot sit below a bronze station with four stable capacitor-cover variants (coils, conductors, twin cells and vents), brushed-metal floor plates and four distinct warm-state room accents: ALPHA gold, BETA coral, DELTA copper and GAMMA lilac, shared by borders, cover highlights, pads, minimap and teleport picker. The final charge darkens the screen edges and pulls electrical streaks inward. Pursuit first builds a rich mint (or violet in the second round) screen tint and soft centre-weighted gradient beneath the existing effects; it eases in over six ticks and fades over the 72-tick release. Above it, pursuit releases a sharp mint-white flash, three stepped pressure fronts with flat translucent gradient wakes (up to 76px on the leading front), five staggered forked lightning columns and outward storm debris. [Charge-release stages](visual-reference/jupiter-pursuit-release.png). Lightning uses a thin 1.35px pale core, a 3px translucent colored middle line and progressively weaker 6/10/15/22px surrounding layers; the release’s large columns also retain very faint broad shoulders and offset echoes; seven softer, separate discharges flicker between the main columns. These quiet bolts are tall and slender (130–174px high), with only small sideways kinks and half-width translucent layers. Four plasma cuts burst from the Reaper. The camera shake lasts 36 ticks at strength 17 and preserves stronger existing shakes. These effects do not alter pursuit speed, damage, storm grace or safe rooms. Entering and leaving pursuit blends palettes over about half a second with a viewport-wide electrical sweep, pressure streaks and edge surge. Pursuit shifts the same geometry to mint, cyan, lavender and teal. Storm rooms have floor tint, fast wind bands (20 px/tick) and large forked lightning columns; the safe room has an inset dashed frame. The minimap outlines the safe room in gold without text and marks storm rooms with /// storm symbols inside the shared square badge style. The Reaper retains his mint-cyan model in both palettes. The energy bar is color-only, with no visible charging/pursuit label or percentage. All text uses the game's existing fonts, with the shared picture-card rules, F card and icon-style C HUD with a single stepped mint lightning bolt with a pale inset, using the block-built shape language of the energy icon. Rule illustrations use the shared yellow player, blue visor and inset Ruby gem; the Volt Fence dash briefly uses the gameplay teal suit and pale visor.

The floor is quiet brushed metal in the original dark bronze (teal during pursuit): each tile is one plate with soft bevels, tiny corner rivets, faint horizontal grain, a diagonal metal sheen on about a quarter of tiles and occasional raised blocky tread. Long conductor strips with sparse polarity markers cross it. It stays darker than cover, leaving the blue lightning-icon hazard tiles distinct. It is cosmetic only.

Charging and pursuit use only the supplied power-up and thunder recordings. Shard Race starts with the supplied Arcade recording; C readiness uses `celestial/skill-ready.wav` at .7; his dash uses the supplied Reaper boost recording at .85 on launch; fence cast and hit use the supplied recordings. Entering each pursuit also plays the supplied thunder-storm recording at .85 volume across the map, once per entry. Charging also plays the supplied power-up recording once. Random, non-overlapping electric ambience clips play in the background, pausing with menus and stopping on exit. No new persistent sound loops. God controls include Charge, Pursuit, Shard Race, Fence Ready (beside Skill Ready) and Phase 1/2/3 (summon him if absent and restart charging in the selected HP phase), styled like the existing controls. All gameplay and C actions pause in rules and menus.

All animated Jupiter lightning shares `drawJupiterLightning`: thin bright cores surrounded by flat translucent color sheets, without blur or thick opaque rectangular strips. It covers distant bolts, wall/floor contacts, storm columns, mini-storm arcs, the Reaper’s charge, overdrive, dash, pickup strikes and melee, orbiting boss sparks, Volt Fence, transition sweeps, the celestial cast’s bolts and player electric chains on this map. The celestial F-cast bolts use 1.8× material thickness (2.43px bright cores), and Volt Fence uses 1.3× thickness (1.76px bright cores); other lightning keeps its normal thin core. Warm, mint, lavender and blue identities stay distinct. Pickup lightning and charge-release explosions reuse this same renderer. Pickup strikes combine a vertical axis-aligned column with that impact cut; room discharge repeats larger cuts across the chamber instead of diagonal zigzag strokes or stretched spokes. Overdrive activation also bursts with mint/white rectangular plasma. [Charging](visual-reference/jupiter-charge-plasma.png) · [Overdrive HUD](visual-reference/jupiter-overdrive-hud.png).

Jupiter environmental electricity uses the same translucent lightning renderer for floor contacts, wall sparks, chamber storms, distant background bolts and transition sweeps. Decorative cloud banks and the storm eye keep their own geometry.

Ambient electricity follows the map palette smoothly: the original warm orange/brass shades outside pursuit, mint green during pursuit. This applies to floor contacts, wall current, background bolts and the transition sweep. Reaper, fragments and mini-storm arcs keep their mint identity; slow-tile electrocution uses blue and white.

From phase 2, reaching full overdrive plays `aegis-attack.mp3` at 1.6 before the attack mix and the Reaper boost recording at 1.7, boosted so it carries over combat. Resetting the bar through a Surge Cell pickup permits a new cue at the next activation; a full bar does not repeat it every tick.
