# The Man Who Never Stops

## The Man in the Storm

On Jupiter, there was an old saying.

“If you see the Reaper running, pray that he isn't running toward you.”

Nobody knew where the saying came from.

Nobody knew who first called him the Storm Reaper, either.

But everyone knew the stories.

For hundreds of years, a mysterious armored figure had been seen racing across Jupiter's floating cities, through storms powerful enough to tear buildings apart.

He wore dark armor, carried an Emerald that glowed with green electricity, and moved faster than any ordinary person.

He never seemed to stop.

Some claimed he was Death, searching the storm for souls. Children said he’d challenged lightning to a race and lost. Ever since, the lightning had been chasing him.

The people of Jupiter had learned to avoid him.

Whenever his green silhouette appeared through the storm clouds, doors were locked, windows were closed, and children were hurried indoors.

Then, inevitably, the lightning would come.

And when the storm passed, the Reaper would be gone.

Among those fascinated by the legend was a young investigator named Renn.

Renn worked for the Jupiter Historical Bureau, an organization devoted to preserving the planet's past.

He had spent years investigating ancient ruins, forgotten settlements, and peculiar local traditions.

But the Reaper was different.

There were photographs of him dating back more than a century.

Stories about him were even older.

Every generation described the same armor, the same green lightning, and the same impossible speed.

Yet Renn noticed something strange.

In one photograph, the Reaper appeared unusually tall.

In another, taken several decades later, he seemed noticeably shorter.

A witness from fifty years ago claimed the Reaper spoke with a woman's voice.

Another insisted he had heard a deep male voice.

Some described him running with a limp.

Others swore his movements were perfectly smooth.

Renn spread the photographs across his desk.

Then he opened his notebook and wrote:

“Possibility One: The Reaper changes his appearance.”

Below it, he added:

“Possibility Two: Witnesses are idiots.”

He considered both explanations.

Neither satisfied him.

So he decided to investigate.

## A Collection of Terrible Theories

Renn began by interviewing witnesses.

His first visit was to an elderly shopkeeper named Doran, who claimed to have seen the Reaper seven times.

“He's collecting the dead,” said Doran.

“Have you actually seen him collect anyone?” asked Renn.

“No,” said Doran.

“Then why do you think that?” asked Renn.

“Look at him,” said Doran.

Renn looked at the photograph.

Admittedly, the armor did not inspire confidence.

A retired pilot had a different theory. “Nobody runs that fast unless they’re frightened.” Renn wrote it down.

By the end of the week, he had collected forty-three accounts.

They contradicted one another in almost every detail.

Except one.

The Reaper always appeared shortly before or during a major storm.

Renn compared the sightings with weather records. The districts the Reaper passed through often took less damage than their neighbors.

Renn decided to follow him.

His first attempt lasted eleven seconds.

The Reaper crossed an entire maintenance platform before Renn had taken his third breath.

His second attempt involved a borrowed hovercraft.

The hovercraft struck a charged floor panel, lost control, and deposited Renn in a fountain.

His third attempt was more successful.

He positioned himself near an abandoned lightning tower where several sightings had been reported.

A powerful storm approached.

Golden fragments began appearing around the old electrical collectors.

Renn had seen them before.

People called them Storm Shards.

Most residents avoided them because touching one could cause a violent electrical discharge.

Then the Reaper appeared.

He moved through the storm with astonishing speed.

Instead of attacking anyone, he raced toward the shards.

One by one, he collected them.

Each fragment disappeared into the Emerald embedded in his armor.

The green glow around him grew brighter.

Then he sprinted toward the next tower.

Renn examined the area.

The electrical readings had dropped dramatically.

He had discovered something important.

But he still had no idea what it meant.

## The Names in the Tower

Renn brought his findings to an engineer named Marae, who maintained Jupiter's aging lightning collectors.

She listened patiently while he explained his investigation.

When he mentioned the golden shards, she immediately became interested.

“You've actually seen him collecting them?” asked Marae.

“Yes,” said Renn.

“Then I think I know what he's doing,” said Marae.

She showed him the original designs of Jupiter's storm-defense network.

Long ago, the floating cities had been protected by enormous lightning collectors.

When dangerous electrical storms approached, these devices intercepted incoming lightning and compressed its energy into glowing Storm Shards.

The system prevented direct strikes from destroying buildings.

But the shards remained dangerous.

If too many accumulated in one place, they could discharge together and cause a catastrophic explosion.

They needed to be removed and transported to isolated discharge towers, where the stored electricity could be safely released into the upper atmosphere.

“So the Reaper is collecting electricity?” asked Renn.

“It appears so,” said Marae.

“Why doesn't he just use a transport ship?” asked Renn.

Marae showed him another diagram.

The Storm Shards became increasingly unstable once collected. Their electromagnetic interference could disable ordinary vehicles.

The ancient Emerald provided a way to contain the energy and convert some of it into power.

But even that protection had limits.

The energy had to be delivered quickly.

Renn remembered the Reaper's extraordinary speed.

Suddenly, the running made sense.

“He's not chasing the storm,” said Renn.

“No,” said Marae.

“He's carrying it away,” said Renn.

Marae nodded.

They searched the tower's abandoned records.

Most had been damaged by centuries of storms.

But in a sealed maintenance room, Renn discovered an old metal cabinet.

Inside was a thick register.

Its cover bore two words.

REAPER RELAY.

Renn opened it.

The pages contained hundreds of names.

Each name had a date beside it.

Some served for only a few months.

Others for decades.

Beside certain names were small notes.

“Retired after injury.”

“Transferred to coastal division.”

“Completed final route.”

Renn turned another page.

He found a photograph of a young woman standing beside the familiar dark armor.

Beneath it was a name.

Kesa — Reaper 47.

Renn stared at the picture.

Then at the list.

Then back at the picture.

The realization struck him.

There had never been one immortal Reaper.

There had been hundreds.

## The Woman Who Used to Run

Kesa was still alive.

She lived in a quiet settlement beyond Jupiter's northern storm belt, where the weather was mild enough for people to spend afternoons outdoors.

Renn found her repairing a small electrical heater.

She was an elderly woman with silver hair and a mechanical brace around one knee.

When he showed her the photograph, she laughed.

“Goodness. I haven't seen that armor in years,” said Kesa.

“You were the Storm Reaper?” asked Renn.

“For fourteen years,” said Kesa.

Renn sat down.

Kesa made tea.

Then she told him the truth.

Centuries ago, Jupiter's first storm runners had been ordinary volunteers.

Their task was to collect unstable Storm Shards and deliver them to isolated discharge towers before the electrical pressure became dangerous.

The Emerald-powered armor allowed its wearer to contain large amounts of energy and survive the journey.

But nobody could perform the work indefinitely.

So the runners created a relay.

One runner would complete a route, transfer the armor and its stored energy to the next, and rest while another continued.

The armor became a symbol of their work.

Eventually, people stopped remembering the individual runners.

They remembered only the figure racing through the storms.

The Storm Reaper.

“Why keep your identities secret?” asked Renn.

“We didn't, at first,” said Kesa.

She showed him an old photograph of the relay team celebrating together.

“But people liked the legend. An immortal guardian protecting Jupiter sounded much more reassuring than a group of exhausted workers taking turns,” said Kesa.

The runners had allowed the story to grow.

It made their work easier in some ways.

People cleared the roads when they saw the armor.

Children stopped playing near dangerous collection platforms.

And occasionally, someone left food for the mysterious guardian.

Kesa smiled.

“That was my favorite part,” said Kesa.

New collectors made the relay quieter for a time. The cities stopped paying to maintain the old towers. When the storms grew worse again, the remaining runners had fewer places to discharge and longer routes between them.

Kesa retired after a severe injury.

Her replacement was a young man named Vale.

“Is he the current Reaper?” asked Renn.

Kesa nodded.

“Unless someone else has taken over,” said Kesa.

Kesa took a map from a drawer. All but one relay route had been crossed out. Renn showed her the tower where he'd seen the Reaper collect the shards. It was on a route marked closed. He pointed out two more sightings from his notebook. Each fell on a different crossed-out line.

“That stubborn idiot,” said Kesa.

“What?” asked Renn.

“He's running all of them,” said Kesa.

## The Last Runner

Renn found Vale during Jupiter's most violent storm in decades.

The floating platforms shook beneath enormous lightning strikes.

Electrical fences flashed across the roads.

Charged floor panels erupted without warning.

And through it all, the Storm Reaper ran.

Renn followed the route Kesa had given him.

He reached an abandoned discharge tower just as the Reaper arrived.

The armored figure stumbled through the entrance.

His Emerald glowed dangerously bright.

He connected himself to the tower's ancient machinery.

A tremendous current surged through the equipment.

Far above, lightning burst harmlessly into the empty upper atmosphere.

The Emerald dimmed.

The Reaper collapsed against the wall.

For several seconds, he did not move.

Then he began struggling to his feet.

Renn stepped forward.

“Vale?” asked Renn.

The Reaper froze.

Slowly, he turned.

“Who told you that name?” asked the Reaper.

“Kesa,” said Renn.

The Reaper sighed.

“Of course she did,” said the Reaper.

He tried to walk past.

Renn blocked the doorway.

“You need to rest,” said Renn.

“Move,” said the Reaper.

“The relay is gone. You can't keep doing this,” said Renn.

“I know,” said the Reaper.

Renn hesitated.

“Then why are you still running?” asked Renn.

Vale looked toward the storm outside.

A distant alarm sounded.

Another collector had reached dangerous pressure.

“Because if I don't, somebody dies,” said Vale.

He pushed past Renn.

But his legs gave way.

Renn caught him before he struck the floor.

For the first time, the Reaper removed his helmet.

There was no immortal spirit beneath it.

No terrifying monster.

Just an exhausted man with tired eyes and a face marked by years of storms.

Vale looked toward the tower's warning display.

“How long until the next discharge?” asked Vale.

“Eleven minutes,” said Renn.

He tried to stand.

Renn held out the old relay register.

“There used to be hundreds of you,” said Renn.

Vale looked at the names.

He recognized several.

Some had trained him.

Others had retired before he was born.

He touched Kesa's name.

“She still complains about my running?” asked Vale.

“Apparently,” said Renn.

For a moment, Vale smiled.

Then the alarm sounded again.

He reached for his helmet.

Renn stopped him.

“You don't have to do every route alone,” said Renn.

“There's nobody left,” said Vale.

Renn looked toward the register.

“Then we'll find some,” said Renn.

## One More Lap

Renn published the old relay records. People were skeptical until families recognized the names and brought out photographs of their own.

Kesa gave an interview.

She described the relay, the Emerald armor, and the years she had spent protecting the cities.

When asked whether she had ever killed anyone with her enormous blades, she looked offended.

“They're for cutting through electrical barriers,” said Kesa.

“Not people?” asked the interviewer.

“Good heavens, no,” said Kesa.

She paused.

“Well, there was one man who tried to steal my lunch.”

Engineers reopened the discharge towers, and volunteers began training in new suits with their own containment packs.

Vale was skeptical.

He had spent so many years running alone that he barely remembered how to trust anyone with the work.

But the new runners trained hard.

Kesa returned to supervise them.

She was merciless.

“You're all too slow,” said Kesa.

“We're running as fast as we can!” said a volunteer.

“Then run faster,” said Kesa.

Renn asked whether she had been this strict with Vale.

“Worse,” said Kesa.

At last, the restored relay was ready.

Its first test came during another enormous storm.

Collectors across Jupiter began filling with golden shards.

Vale put on his armor.

The Emerald glowed.

He launched into the storm.

His speed was as extraordinary as ever, but he was exhausted before reaching the first tower.

He prepared to continue.

Then someone stepped into his path.

A young woman wearing newly constructed relay armor.

Her name was Jori.

She held out her hand.

“Your turn's over,” said Jori.

Vale stared at her.

“You don't understand. I have to keep moving,” said Vale.

“I know,” said Jori.

She activated her equipment.

“That's why I'm here.”

Vale hesitated.

Then he transferred the collected energy into her containment pack.

Jori secured it and sprinted toward the next tower.

Another runner waited farther down the road. Before long, the discharge tower lit the clouds green.

For the first time in years, Vale watched someone else complete the route.

He slowly sat down on the tower steps.

Renn joined him.

They watched the runners disappear into the storm.

“So, what does the legendary Storm Reaper do when he isn't running?” asked Renn.

Vale considered the question.

For the first time in years, he had no immediate answer.

Then his stomach growled.

Loudly.

Renn looked at him.

Vale looked embarrassed.

“I haven't eaten since yesterday,” said Vale.

“We could start with dinner,” said Renn.

Vale took off his helmet and walked with him toward the city.
