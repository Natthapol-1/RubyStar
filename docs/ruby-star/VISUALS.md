# Visual style

[Overview](README.md)

**Use the luminous square player and the full-energy aura as the baseline.** New visuals should belong beside the current player, Solar, Sirius and Singularity. Keep silhouettes crisp and blocky, glow selective, and detail restrained. Avoid blurred bodies, smooth curvy attack splashes and flat faceted replacement models.

## Style: blocky neon-vector

Ruby Star is **blocky neon-vector** art: solid flat-colored geometric shapes (mostly rectangles and squares) with hard edges on a dark space background, a limited palette per object, and glow only on rims, cores and key accents. It is vector, not pixel art: shapes are not tied to a pixel grid.

- **At rest, stay blocky and grid-aligned.** Bodies, panels and parts are axis-aligned rectangles with square corners and stepped (not curved) outlines.
- **Moving parts rotate and scale as whole solid shapes.** A spinning blade, beam or plate is one rectangle or polygon transformed as a unit, so it keeps clean straight edges at every angle (Reaper blades, player and enemy lasers).
- **Never build a diagonal or rotated element as a stepped pixel staircase.** Use a rotated solid shape or a straight stroked band instead; staircases look jagged and blurry in motion.
- **Glow is light and selective** (about 2–6px), never a haze that defines a silhouette.

## Shape, shading and glow recipe

1. Build the main silhouette from clean axis-aligned rectangles and bold stair steps. Keep square corners and a readable face at actual gameplay scale. For circles/planets/waves, use block-built stepped boundaries. The Saturn suit hoop keeps its tilted octagon shape but is traced in grid-snapped square blocks.
2. Use a luminous colored rim, a flat darker inset, a few medium shade patches and a pale core/highlight. Keep the solid silhouette fully readable without the glow. The rim and inset should remain uninterrupted enough to read as a simple square.
3. Apply soft colored glow around the rim and a few selected energy accents. Keep the actual fill pixels and steps sharp. Do not blur the whole sprite, use a huge haze to create its silhouette, or glow every adjacent cell independently. Sparse separated sparks preserve negative space.
4. Vary opacity and lightness within one established color family. Eye visibility comes from light/dark contrast; it need not use a complementary hue. Preserve explicit exceptions such as Lunar's mint visor.
5. Use `drawFullEnergyAura` and `drawEnergyPixelRay` as motion baselines: staggered elongated cardinal rays, stepped diagonal sparks, small separated fragments. Quantize positions with `Math.round`/`Math.floor`; use stable geometry rather than fresh random jitter every frame.
6. Preserve an individual effect identity. Shared construction and glow do not mean every cast gets identical rings, spokes or the same central motif. Show anticipation, release, travel/field and fade where appropriate. Existing cast pose compresses/releases over 24 ticks and never locks player input.
7. Match visual footprints to actual damage/slow tiles. Use the same helpers/bounds for membership and drawing. Clip fields to floor and leave walls clear. Never change collision/range to make a cosmetic preview easier.

Useful render helpers in `RubyStarGame.tsx`: `drawPlayer`, `drawMobBody`, `drawEnemy`, `drawFullEnergyAura`, `drawEnergyPixelRay`, `drawCelestialCast`, `drawPixelBeam`, `drawMobBrackets`, `drawBossHealthBar`, `drawBattlefield`, `drawRewardSpark`. Extend these patterns rather than inventing a second art system.

## Celestial cast and field identities

- **Solar:** round stepped sun disc (red rim, orange body, pale core) ringed by twelve flickering corona tongues, plus eight tapered rectangle-built flame streams with side tongues/bright tips. Cast lasts 48 ticks; red tint uses same fade. Screen shake 14 ticks, strength 6. Keep flames readable rather than a white blurred explosion. Never use Sirius's cross-shaped star as Solar's core.
- **Lunar:** visible 54-tick purple crescent/ring/ray cast. Decoy is an 80%-opacity copy of Lunar suit, 95% on hit and 50–70% in invulnerability. It must remain readable across the battlefield.
- **Jupiter:** the simplified recipe: a 12px stepped banded orange orb, one blocky ring expanding to the 6-tile reach and four stepped 12px lightning bolts on the cardinal axes. No spirals, trails or glow. Shake 18 ticks, strength 8.
- **Neptune:** the simplified recipe: a 12px stepped blue orb with its dark spot, two blocky rings expanding to the 8-tile push reach and three pale plus-shaped heal crosses rising above. No foam, chevrons or glow; it differs from Jupiter by its cool palette, double ring and crosses.
- **Sirius:** cross-shaped yellow/white star with long tapering cardinal diffraction spikes, short diagonal sparks, a twinkling ring of twelve sparkles and a small white-dwarf companion orbiting on a flattened path. Bright yellow screen flash fades quadratically over 21 ticks with peak opacity 0.42.
- **Gaia:** enlarged (1.3×) Earth, roots, leaves and sprouts; roots start at the planet's edge. Branching right-angle roots, sparse green growth and varied tile opacity. Do not copy Lunar's rings/radial stars. Field has crisp 4px exposed-tile edges and excludes walls.
- **Singularity:** black core/silver rim that grows with the cast, an expanding stepped grey horizon ring, grey fragments moving inward, visible affected tiles. The void core stays opaque longer than its silver glow so it never fades into a grey haze. Exposed field boundary uses the same 4px geometry and pulsing opacity as Gaia, in grey. Motion points inward rather than outward.
- **Venus:** a cloud-veiled pearl planet with cream/rose chevrons inside two expanding square mirror frames of 12px plates (narrow gaps, sweeping white glints) and four diagonal sparkles; no round rings. Also cast on placed Ruby. Active veil: one 4px square shell of alternating pearl/rose mirror plates around the player (.68 tile, .88 while carrying), one sweeping white glint and four corner sparkles; it blinks during the last 2s. Ruby still receives the veil's protection, but has no second shell. Reflected hits travel as short pearl pixel streaks from defender to attacker and end in a small cross flash.
- **Binary Star:** coral upper and blue lower half-rings expand together while a coral and a blue star orbit apart from a white seam, trailing blocks. Also cast where the twin appears. Active twin: a 62%-opacity copy of the Binary suit with a faint blue square wash on the tile beside Ruby, linked to the player by a dotted coral-to-blue line with a white pulse; both blink during the last 2s. Minimap marks the twin with a blue square. Twin attacks use the normal player laser/bullet/wave/bomb visuals.
- **Saturn:** continuous polygon hoop with depth (rear before body/front after), broad stepped cast bands (orbit 72px growing by 42px, planet at 1.2×), sparse pale moving glints. Active guard has faceted turquoise shell, faint interior and four orbiting lights; shield radii are player .62 tile, placed Ruby .7, carried Ruby .82, with 4px shell pixels.

Broad stepped cast rings generally use 12px rectangle steps, light/dark halves, clear gaps and sparse highlights. Their width should not erase the central planet. Preserve stronger overlapping screen shake rather than resetting it to a weaker one.
