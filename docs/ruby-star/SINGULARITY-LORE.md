# The Universe Is Not a Buffet

## A Scientific Breakthrough

At the edge of known space stood the Institute of Extremely Important Questions, a research station suspended above two miniature black holes.

The holes drifted beneath its observation chambers, slowly circling through magnetic restraints. The staff called them Maw One and Maw Two. Nobody remembered who had named them, although the official designation Controlled Gravitational Anomalies A and B remained on the doors in increasingly small print.

Professor Orlo, the institute's director, considered the Maws his life's work.

His assistant, Vexa, considered them two excellent reasons to work somewhere else.

Everything changed when a salvage vessel delivered a creature found inside a derelict ship. It was round, dark, and mostly mouth. In the center of its body glimmered an Obsidian, black as the space between stars. Its surface bent the surrounding light, tugging loose screws and crumbs toward the creature whenever it opened its jaws.

Orlo named it the Soul Devourer.

Vexa suggested Do Not Open Container.

The creature ate the label before they could settle the disagreement.

During its first examination, it consumed a measuring tape, two pencils, a chair leg, and the written results of its first examination. It attempted to swallow Orlo's pocket watch but spat it out when the alarm rang.

Orlo was delighted.

“A creature of limitless curiosity!” said Orlo.

“It's eating your desk,” said Vexa.

“Then it has excellent taste,” said Orlo.

The researchers soon discovered that the Devourer had a particular appetite for Lost Souls: harmless pale spirits that wandered through the station's gravity fields. Most drifted between rooms, glowing softly and bothering no one. The institute had kept a population of them for decades because nobody knew where they came from and everybody liked watching them float.

A Lost Soul approached the containment glass.

The Devourer pressed its enormous mouth against the other side.

The soul floated away.

Orlo took this as evidence of hunger rather than a warning.

He summoned the staff for what he announced would be the greatest feeding experiment in scientific history.

Vexa asked whether anyone had investigated what happened after the creature ate.

Orlo checked his notes.

The creature had eaten those too.

## The Worst Feeding Experiment in History

The first experiment took place at precisely nine in the morning.

Orlo opened a hatch and released one Lost Soul into the containment room. The Devourer lunged, swallowed it, and shuddered. Its Obsidian flashed. The creature swelled like an overfilled balloon.

Then it became two creatures.

For several seconds, the laboratory was silent.

“Professor,” said Vexa.

“Remarkable,” said Orlo.

“There are two,” said Vexa.

“Twice as remarkable,” said Orlo.

Orlo concluded that the Devourer had been lonely and had made itself a friend. He ordered another soul delivered so that both specimens could be studied after a meal.

Vexa objected.

The first Devourer ate the soul and divided again.

A technician dropped his clipboard. One of the creatures ate it.

Another Devourer tried to eat the technician's shoe. He escaped, but left the shoe behind. It was entered into the official inventory as temporarily missing.

Orlo proposed a larger feeding trial.

Vexa pointed at the three Devourers now climbing the equipment shelves.

“Every soul makes another mouth,” said Vexa.

“Then we'll need more food,” said Orlo.

“That makes MORE mouths,” said Vexa.

“And therefore more data,” said Orlo.

Before Vexa could respond, an intern entered carrying a gold-glowing Lost Soul in a reinforced lantern.

Golden Souls were extremely rare. Nobody had ever managed to explain their bright hearts or the little sparks orbiting them.

Orlo looked at the lantern with the expression of a man about to win a prize.

Vexa looked at him with the expression of a woman considering early retirement.

“Do not feed it the gold one,” said Vexa.

“I wasn't going to,” said Orlo.

The intern stumbled over the missing shoe.

The lantern flew open.

One Devourer gulped down the Golden Soul.

Its body flashed gold, raced around the chamber, and split into three.

The new copies immediately scattered in different directions, as if each had an urgent appointment with a different piece of furniture.

One ate the emergency manual.

One ate a fire extinguisher and developed the hiccups.

The third discovered the laboratory door and demonstrated that it was less durable than advertised.

A siren began blaring.

“Containment breach. Please remain calm,” said an automated voice.

A Devourer ate the speaker.

“This is a tremendous success!” said Orlo.

Vexa threw a chair at him.

The largest Devourer caught it in its mouth.

There were now enough specimens to justify evacuating the floor, which Orlo described as excellent experimental participation.

## Emergency Measures

By noon, the Devourers had escaped into the residential sector.

They preferred Lost Souls, chasing their pale lights through every corridor. Between meals, however, they tested the edibility of anything in reach. Each copy developed its own interests. One chewed wires. Another collected doorknobs. One refused to eat anything except office furniture, causing a short-lived shortage of chairs.

The station's security chief, Captain Brann, attempted an arrest.

“You are surrounded,” said Brann.

The Devourer ate his handcuffs.

“That was station property,” said Brann.

It ate his badge.

Brann decided to prioritize evacuation.

Orlo's first containment plan involved a giant food dispenser designed to gather the swarm in one place. The device worked beautifully. Every Devourer hurried toward it.

Then they ate the souls released inside.

The number of Devourers doubled.

Orlo called the dispenser a success because it had attracted all the specimens.

Vexa disconnected it with an axe.

The second plan involved reinforced blast doors. The creatures did not bother chewing through them. They found a ventilation shaft and emerged from the ceiling of the emergency meeting room.

The meeting was adjourned when one ate the agenda.

The third plan involved waiting for the creatures to grow tired.

Surprisingly, this worked.

After going without Lost Souls for a while, the Devourers grew pale and sluggish. Their Obsidian glow dimmed. They wandered aimlessly and seemed almost too weak to hold themselves upright.

Brann cautiously approached one with a capture net.

A Lost Soul drifted out of a broken ventilation duct.

Every Devourer in sight snapped awake.

The nearest one swallowed it and split. The new copy shot down the corridor, scattering tools and three guards who had made the mistake of standing in a straight line.

Brann climbed out of a storage bin.

“Why does the station have so many ghosts?” asked Brann.

“Because until today they were the least dangerous things here,” said Vexa.

At three in the afternoon, the institute lost communication with the navigation deck.

At four, the navigation deck reported that it had been eaten.

At five, Orlo announced a breakthrough: the creatures were not malicious.

Vexa agreed. That was the worst part. They had no plan, no grudge, no villainous ambition. They wanted only their next meal, and each meal made the problem bigger.

By six, there were Devourers in the walls.

Orlo's second lunch was gone.

He demanded an inquiry.

## A Very Hungry Black Hole

The answer came from an accident, which Vexa privately considered the institute's most reliable research method.

She was escorting a group of harmless Lost Souls away from the observation chambers when a starving Devourer followed their glow into the gravity field of Maw One.

The creature struggled as the black hole pulled it across the floor.

It stretched its jaws wider and wider, attempting to bite the darkness itself.

Vexa watched in disbelief.

The Devourer reached the center.

It vanished.

No splitting. No explosion. No extra mouths.

Just a tiny flash and a noise like someone finishing the last sip of a drink.

Vexa checked the chamber twice.

Then she smiled.

For the first time all day, something had eaten a Devourer instead.

Before she left, Vexa tested the result with a second starving copy. It followed a faint Lost Soul toward the Maw, reached the edge of the field, and immediately tried to retreat. Vexa nudged the gravity regulator forward. The creature slid another step, clamped its teeth around a floor bolt, and took the bolt with it when the Maw swallowed it.

Vexa checked the instruments. The number of Devourers had decreased. The number of floor bolts had also decreased, but the station could afford one of those losses more easily than the other.

She wrote a note on her sleeve: Black holes: exceptionally effective. Also extremely rude to floors.

She hurried to the control room and found Orlo explaining to Brann how an emergency buffet could improve morale.

“We can get rid of them,” said Vexa.

“With seasoning?” asked Orlo.

“With the Maws. They swallow the creatures without allowing them to multiply,” said Vexa.

Orlo looked wounded.

“Those are valuable research specimens,” said Orlo.

“They've eaten the research,” said Vexa.

She outlined a plan. The crew would lure the Lost Souls away from inhabited sections, cut off the swarm's meals, and wait for the Devourers to weaken. Then gravity controls could pull them toward the two black holes.

Any remaining souls would be guided near the outer edges of the Maw fields as bait, then collected before the creatures reached them.

Orlo raised his hand.

“What if we simply feed the black holes until they're full?” asked Orlo.

Vexa stared.

“Do you know what a black hole is?” asked Vexa.

“A question worth investigating,” said Orlo.

Brann confiscated Orlo's access card.

They named the operation DO NOT FEED IT.

Orlo submitted a written objection to the name.

A Devourer ate the objection.

## Operation Do Not Feed It

The plan required engineers, pilots, security officers, and the remaining researchers to work together.

It also required Orlo not to touch anything.

Vexa wrote this instruction on a sign large enough to be read from the opposite end of the control room.

Orlo stood beside it, visibly offended.

The crew activated the gravity controls. Along the corridors, pale guide lamps drew Lost Souls into shielded holding bays. Brann's officers checked every door twice, chiefly because several doors were now missing their handles.

The Devourers grew hungry.

Their color faded. They staggered after distant wisps, too tired to resist the pull of the two Maws.

Vexa engaged the first Gravity Surge.

Three creatures slid across the chamber toward Maw One.

The first vanished.

The second tried to eat the first and vanished too.

The third opened its mouth toward the black hole, reconsidered too late, and disappeared with an offended squeak.

Cheers erupted across the control room.

Then Orlo found an unmarked button.

He pressed it.

Gravity reversed.

Vexa struck the ceiling. Brann's helmet floated away. An entire desk drifted past the window, closely followed by the Devourer that had spent the afternoon eating desks.

“WHAT DID THE SIGN SAY?” asked Vexa.

“Nothing about buttons!” said Orlo.

The sign floated past them.

It said DO NOT TOUCH ANYTHING.

Orlo considered this a matter of interpretation.

Brann restored the gravity before Vexa could demonstrate a second use for her axe.

Below, Maw Two began its periodic gulp, its field swelling to engulf most of the adjacent chamber. Starving Devourers skidded across the floor. Some tried to run; others seemed convinced that the glowing center was an unusually large meal.

The station shuddered as the swarm dwindled.

Then a Golden Soul slipped through an unsecured maintenance hatch.

One Devourer spotted it.

The creature surged forward, swallowed the soul, and split into three fast-moving copies.

“We're back where we started!” said Brann.

“No. We know where they need to go,” said Vexa.

She directed the crew to open the chamber's outer gravity vents. The newly fed copies raced down the corridor, chasing the remaining Lost Souls. Brann's team redirected the spirits into safe holding bays just before the Devourers arrived.

Suddenly there was nothing left to eat.

The copies prowled, impatient, then slowed.

Vexa pushed one toward Maw One. A technician pulled another into its field. The third resisted until the Maw gulped again and swallowed it whole.

The counters fell.

Five creatures.

Three.

One.

The final Devourer was the largest, its Obsidian shining like a dark star. It had somehow found Orlo's third lunch and was chewing the container thoughtfully.

Orlo watched in horror.

“That was a very expensive sandwich,” said Orlo.

Vexa smiled for the first time since morning.

“Professor, I need you to do something reckless,” said Vexa.

Orlo straightened.

“At last, recognition,” said Orlo.

She handed him an empty lunch box and pointed toward Maw Two.

Orlo approached the field, waving the box.

The Devourer followed. Its Obsidian flickered against the enormous pull, and its jaws opened wide enough to swallow Orlo, the lunch box, and most of the railing.

At the last moment, Vexa hauled the professor backward with a gravity tether.

The Devourer lunged after him.

Its momentum carried it over the Maw's center.

For one magnificent instant, the hungriest creature in the station appeared to be trying to swallow a black hole.

Then the black hole swallowed it.

Silence settled over the control room.

Orlo examined his crushed lunch box.

“Do you suppose there's any chance of getting the sandwich back?” asked Orlo.

## The Final Report

By morning, the station was safe enough to begin repairs.

The Lost Souls were released into the observation gardens. The Maws drifted quietly beneath their chambers, apparently unaffected by having eaten an entire scientific disaster.

The damage report listed forty-seven doors and twenty-nine desks. The whereabouts of the emergency manual remained unknown.

Captain Brann requested a transfer. His application listed his preferred destination as "anywhere with doors." When human resources asked him to clarify, he wrote "doors that remain attached."

Orlo requested a grant.

Vexa requested a vacation, preferably on a planet without mouths.

At the first staff meeting, Orlo presented his official conclusion.

Experiment 247: Successfully Determined the Maximum Appetite of the Soul Devourer.

“We didn't determine its maximum appetite,” said Vexa.

“Of course we did,” said Orlo.

“We threw it into a black hole,” said Vexa.

“And it stopped eating, didn't it?” asked Orlo.

Vexa considered throwing something at him, but the station had very few chairs left.

Then an alarm rang from the sealed containment wing.

A small dark creature sat inside a forgotten specimen locker. Its mouth occupied most of its body. A tiny black gleam shimmered beneath its skin.

It ate the locker label.

Orlo pressed his face against the glass.

“Extraordinary. A juvenile specimen!” said Orlo.

“Don't,” said Vexa.

“Perhaps a controlled feeding experiment—” said Orlo.

“NO,” said Vexa.

The baby Devourer ate the latch.

Vexa reached for the emergency gravity switch. Orlo reached for his notebook.

So did the Devourer.
