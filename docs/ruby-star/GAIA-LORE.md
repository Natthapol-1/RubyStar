# The Surprise Party for a Rock

## A Very Important Discovery

By the time the people of Gaia realized they were celebrating the wrong birthday, the cake was already in the oven.

The village voted to continue.

After all, it would have been rude to cancel.

The trouble had begun that morning when Pip, a mushroom seller with an impressive collection of hats and a considerably less impressive understanding of geology, discovered a large boulder in the middle of the village road.

He rarely paid attention to rocks unless something was growing on them. This one had three grooves. If a tree’s rings could tell you its age, Pip saw no reason a rock’s grooves couldn’t. The rock was turning three.

Pip hurried home, wrapped a particularly fine mushroom in colorful paper, and returned to present it to the birthday boulder.

Unfortunately, the road keeper arrived before he could finish his speech.

“Why is there a rock in the road?” asked the road keeper.

“It's his birthday,” said Pip.

“Whose birthday?” asked the road keeper.

“The rock's,” said Pip.

The grooves were tire marks. The road keeper brought over his cart to demonstrate, and the wheels fit perfectly.

“Remarkable,” said Pip.

“So you see, the rock isn't three years old,” said the road keeper.

“Of course,” said Pip.

“Good,” said the road keeper.

“How old is the cart?” asked Pip.

The road keeper decided this was a conversation better left unfinished.

Unfortunately, Pip had already produced another invitation.

He asked whether the cart would like to attend the party.

The road keeper hesitated.

He had lived in Gaia for twenty-seven years and had never once been invited to a celebration by a mushroom seller.

He accepted on the cart's behalf.

By noon the baker had offered to make a cake and the village clerk had put up two signs.

ROAD CLOSED FOR ROCK.

The second read:

PLEASE USE OTHER ROAD.

Gaia had only one road.

The clerk put the second sign on the opposite side of the boulder, where it would be somebody else’s problem. When she asked for a name for the paperwork, Pip tried several. A leaf blew off the rock while he was calling it Arthur. Pip took that as a vote for Bernard.

The clerk prepared a banner reading HAPPY BIRTHDAY, leaving a blank space at the end in case the boulder eventually decided.

By then, everyone had become rather attached to Bernard.

Nobody asked the rock whether it minded.

## Preparations for the Century

The baker made a cake large enough for the whole village. Nobody knew how much a rock could eat, she explained. The children entered Bernard in musical chairs. He was eliminated in the first round, but the clerk granted a special exception allowing him cake.

Meanwhile, the road keeper tried to wrap Bernard's birthday present.

He had purchased a small decorative stone from the village market.

When Pip asked why he had bought a rock for a rock, the road keeper explained that it was a paperweight.

“What would Bernard need to weigh down?” asked Pip.

“His papers,” said the road keeper.

“Of course,” said Pip.

Neither man considered that Bernard did not possess any papers.

The Thorn Weaver volunteered to decorate. She could grow an arch of flowers in seconds. Her vines were excellent at following instructions and much less skilled at understanding what she meant.

The Weaver arrived carrying ribbons, flowers, and an enormous pair of pruning shears.

The cutting blade was formed from her Peridot, which made the tool powerful enough to slice through even the toughest enchanted vines.

When she saw a child running past with the cake knife, she decided it would be safer to remove the Peridot blade and keep it in her belt.

She placed the remaining handles on the refreshment table.

Then she began decorating.

At first, everything went beautifully.

Vines curled into arches above the road.

Flowers blossomed around the tables.

A magnificent wreath formed around Bernard.

The villagers applauded.

The Weaver beamed.

Encouraged by her success, she held up an enormous roll of ribbon.

“Now, wrap everything!” said the Thorn Weaver.

It was a simple instruction.

Perhaps too simple.

One vine took the ribbon.

Another took the table.

A third wrapped itself around the baker's ankles.

Within seconds, the entire refreshment area began rising into the air.

The Weaver blinked.

“Wait, I meant—”

A vine wrapped around her mouth.

The Weaver made a furious noise.

The vines interpreted this as enthusiasm.

They worked even harder.

## The Most Beautiful Disaster

By late afternoon, the village looked magnificent.

It was also almost entirely inaccessible.

Flowers covered the houses, the road keeper’s cart hung from a tree, and the clerk’s office looked like a birthday present.

The baker was hanging upside down beside her cake.

The cake itself remained perfectly level.

This was because she had managed to convince a vine that it was carrying a very full cup of tea.

The vine took this responsibility extremely seriously.

It would not tilt the cake under any circumstances.

The baker considered this a small victory.

Children got past the vines by holding up wrapped presents. The vines added bows and let them through. The clerk wrote “This party has exceeded its permitted area” on an invitation after her desk was tied to her chair.

Meanwhile, Pip was having the time of his life.

He had climbed onto Bernard to avoid the vines and was now wearing three party hats.

He considered the situation a tremendous success.

“Look at all these decorations!” said Pip.

“My cart is in a tree!” said the road keeper.

“Yes! It's beautiful!” said Pip.

The road keeper stared at his cart.

Someone had tied a ribbon to each wheel.

He had to admit it looked quite nice.

The Thorn Weaver, however, was becoming increasingly frustrated.

She had managed to bite through the vine covering her mouth.

This gave her approximately one second to issue a corrective command.

“STOP!” said the Thorn Weaver.

Every vine froze.

Unfortunately, they had frozen while holding everything.

The baker stayed upside down, the cart stayed in the tree, and the handles of the Weaver’s shears stayed on the refreshment table, thirty feet up.

For the first time in her life, the Thorn Weaver wished she had learned to decorate with ordinary string.

## A Very Complicated Rescue

The road keeper climbed the nearest tree to retrieve the shears. The tree wrapped him in a ribbon. The Weaver tried to choose her next words carefully.

“Lower the road keeper gently to the ground,” said the Thorn Weaver.

The vines obeyed.

They lowered him headfirst until his helmet touched the dirt.

Then they stopped.

“Could I be the other way up?” asked the road keeper.

The vines turned him around.

Unfortunately, they turned the entire tree with him.

Eventually he got down with the handles. The Peridot blade was still in the Weaver’s belt, and every time she reached for it a vine offered her a ribbon.

At last, the baker suggested giving the vines a new task.

“Tell them to prepare the table for cake,” said the baker.

The Weaver looked skeptical.

“They're very good with cake,” said the baker.

This was difficult to dispute.

The Weaver instructed the vines to lower the refreshment table carefully and release anyone preventing the guests from eating.

The table descended.

The baker was freed.

One villager had managed to eat a slice while hanging upside down. He landed safely and looked at his plate.

“Could you put me back for a moment? I left the jam up there.”

Nobody helped him.

With the crowd finally untangled, the Thorn Weaver retrieved her Peridot blade and fitted it into the shears.

The tool glowed bright green.

She began cutting through the remaining vines.

The task went much more smoothly once she stopped trying to issue commands.

Within an hour, the houses were free, the bridge was clear, and most villagers had been returned to approximately where they belonged.

The road keeper found his cart waiting beside the road.

Its wheels were decorated with colorful ribbons.

He examined them carefully.

“Are you going to take those off?” asked Pip.

“Not yet,” said the road keeper.

“Why?” asked Pip.

“I paid a lot for this cart. It's never looked this cheerful,” said the road keeper.

He pulled it forward.

The ribbons made a soft slapping sound against the wheels.

The road keeper smiled.

## The Guest of Honor

By evening the clerk had recorded a road closure, an accidental forest expansion and an unauthorized celebration. At the bottom she wrote, “No complaints received from guest of honor.”

Bernard was still wrapped in several enormous layers of ribbon.

The villagers gathered around him.

The Thorn Weaver approached with her newly repaired shears.

“Shall I cut him free?” asked the Thorn Weaver.

Pip raised his hand.

“Wait!” said Pip.

Everyone turned.

“We have to keep it a surprise,” said Pip.

The road keeper looked at the boulder.

“He's been here all day,” said the road keeper.

“Exactly. Imagine how surprised he'll be when the party starts,” said Pip.

The baker pointed out that half the cake had already been eaten.

Pip considered this.

“A smaller surprise, then,” said Pip.

The children took their places with the presents, the baker held up what was left of the cake, and the clerk counted down.

The Weaver cut the ribbon.

For a moment, nothing happened.

Then Bernard moved.

The enormous boulder began rolling downhill.

The villagers stood frozen.

The road keeper gasped.

“MY ROAD!” said the road keeper.

Bernard rolled straight through PLEASE USE OTHER ROAD and disappeared around the bend.

Pip was the first to speak.

“Well. He certainly enjoyed that,” said Pip.

The Thorn Weaver looked at the empty patch of road.

Something was still there.

A much smaller rock sat in the shallow depression where Bernard had been resting.

It was round, smooth, and no larger than a loaf of bread.

The baker slowly approached.

She looked at the little rock.

Then she looked at the remaining cake.

Her face went pale.

“Oh no,” said the baker.

“What?” asked Pip.

“We forgot his birthday,” said the baker.

The village went silent.

The clerk reached for her notebook.

The road keeper sat down.

The children immediately began arguing over whether the little rock was one year old or zero years old.

Pip examined it carefully.

There were no grooves.

“It's a newborn!” said Pip.

The Thorn Weaver looked at the villagers reaching for the wrapping paper and put Bernard’s hat on the little rock. Then a voice rose from down the hill.

“THANK YOU FOR THE PARTY!”

Pip dropped his plate. The road keeper looked at his cart.

“Well?”

The cart said nothing.

## An Unexpected Guest

The clerk opened a new page and wrote INCIDENT: POSSIBLE TALKING ROCK. Then she crossed out possible.

“EXCELLENT CAKE, BY THE WAY!”

The baker looked delighted.

“He liked it!” said the baker.

“He didn't eat any,” said the road keeper.

“Perhaps he could smell it,” said the baker.

“Do rocks have noses?” asked Pip.

The road keeper went down the hill to investigate, on the grounds that it was a matter of road safety. He came back with two pieces of news. Bernard had always been able to speak, and he wanted more cake.

“We spent an hour asking him what his name was!” said Pip.

“He said he liked listening to the suggestions,” said the road keeper.

“Then why did he move?” asked Pip.

The road keeper hesitated.

“Apparently, he was trying to get comfortable,” said the road keeper.

Everyone looked toward the little rock still sitting in the road.

A long silence followed.

The Thorn Weaver slowly bent down.

She examined it.

Then she called toward the hill.

“Bernard?” asked the Thorn Weaver.

“YES?” asked Bernard.

“Is this little rock yours?” asked the Thorn Weaver.

There was a pause.

“OH! THERE HE IS!” said Bernard.

Pip gasped.

“You lost your child?” asked Pip.

“NO, I WAS SITTING ON HIM,” said Bernard.

The entire village fell silent.

The road keeper pressed a hand against his forehead.

“Of course you were,” said the road keeper.

Bernard continued cheerfully.

“I WAS WONDERING WHERE HE'D GONE.”

The Weaver carried the little rock down the hill. The village followed with what was left of the cake.

“THANK YOU,” said Bernard.

“You're welcome,” said Pip.

Pip hesitated.

“By the way, how old are you actually?”

Bernard thought about this.

For quite a long time.

“I DON'T REMEMBER,” said Bernard.

Pip's face lit up.

“Then it's a good thing we celebrated!” said Pip.

Bernard asked if they could do it again next year. The baker said yes, the clerk said no, and the Weaver suggested someone else decorate.

On the way home, the road keeper leaned toward his cart. “If you’ve been able to talk this whole time, I’d appreciate knowing now.”

The cart stayed quiet until a ribbon caught under one wheel. As the road keeper bent to untangle it, something creaked behind him.

“I liked the ribbon.”

The road keeper straightened his hat. “Fine. You can keep it.”
