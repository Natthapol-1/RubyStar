# Binary Star challenge

[Overview](README.md) · [Gameplay](GAMEPLAY.md#modes-and-lobby)

Regular alien spawns use the [shared challenge pool](ENEMIES.md#shared-challenge-alien-pool): Ghost, Bomber and Lancer join after 60 seconds, with Shooter rare. The map’s themed enemies keep their own replacement chances and phase rules.

`/challenge/binary` is a fight against the Astral Twins in which the pair has three lives, jumps between opposite rooms, and is pushed and pulled by your magnetic pole. Everything shared by challenges (stage 0, guardian-only boss, recharging celestial skill, win on defeat) is in [Gameplay](GAMEPLAY.md). Tuning lives in `BINARY` in `binary.ts`; logic is `binary.ts` (`state.binary`, with short hooks in `engine.ts`), art is `binary-art.ts`, the rule sheet is `BinaryGuide.tsx` and the C icon is `MagnetStatus.tsx`. Its world art is `binary-world-art.ts`.

Every phase is built around one rule, matching poles push and opposite poles pull, and around Binary Collision: shove one twin into the other for a big opening. Collision is a reward, not a requirement: the twins can be beaten with ordinary attacks. Phase 1 has you, your Binary Star twin (F), the twins, Lodestones and Pulsars. Phase 2 adds room magnets and Grounders. Phase 3 adds Pole Reversal and charging tiles.

The Binary Star book button beside Begin Challenge opens [The Space Between Us](BINARY-LORE.md), the tale of two communities and the Astral Twins.

## Twins and lives

- Dawn (melee, coral) and Dusk (ranged, blue) keep their [Endless behaviour](ENEMIES.md#boss-encounters), including HP sharing and the 12-second revival window, except that a revived twin returns with 40% of its life instead of 60% (`reviveHpRatio`), and only 25% from that twin's second revival in the same phase (`lateReviveHpRatio`; the count restarts each phase), and they share HP only every 15–18 seconds instead of 6–9 (`transferDelay`).
- **Berserk** is much harsher here (`berserk`, via `twinBerserk` in `engine.ts`): while its partner is down the survivor moves 1.6× as fast, attacks with 0.4× the cooldown (its new moves included) and deals 1.5× damage (Endless: 1.25× speed, 0.6× cooldown, normal damage). It still takes 65% damage. Finishing them separately is meant to be risky.
- **No revival while stunned:** a downed twin's revival timer does not count down while it is stunned (a collision or wall-slam stun it had when it fell); the HUD's draining bar holds still until the stun ends.
- Each phase is one life: each twin has 1650 HP (`twinHP`). Defeating both twins (the second within the revival window) costs the pair a life: both rise at full HP in the next phase, with a revive effect, a PHASE n banner and a 3-second attack grace. Defeating both in phase 3 wins.
- They arrive the same way: when the pair spawns, each jumps in to one of two diagonally opposite chambers. Every 35 seconds in phases 1–2 and every 45 seconds in phase 3 (`swapTicks`) the twins split into two diagonally opposite chambers (ALPHA ↔ GAMMA or BETA ↔ DELTA), facing each other across the station (`jumpRooms`). They take the opposite pair that does not hold you, so they flank you from the neighbouring rooms (a random pair while you are in a hallway), and each prefers a room it is not already in. A lone survivor jumps to the chamber opposite its fallen partner, which stays where it fell. They land at least 3 tiles from you when possible. For the last 1.5 seconds flashing corner brackets in each twin's colour close in, and the screen edges blink coral and blue in turn, faster as the jump nears (`drawBinaryScreen`), starting with the Solar Flare swoosh (`celestial/solar-flare-swoosh.wav`); the jump itself gives a short pale screen flash with coral top/left and blue bottom/right edges. The jump plays the teleport sound boosted to 1.6 (`jumpVolume`, 2.5× the player's teleport) so it carries across the map, layered with `celestial/jupiter-storm.wav` at .9 (`jumpStormVolume`).
- Meteors always take 10% of a twin's life (165 HP), ignoring the bond and a berserk survivor's damage reduction.

## Binary Collision

- **Bound:** while the twins are not exposed, they are bound, shown by a dashed line in their two colours joining them. Ordinary hits on them deal 60% (`boundDamage`): your attacks and your F copy's. Collisions ignore the bond.
- **Collision:** a twin moved by any magnetic force (a field pulse from you or your F twin, or a swap shove) that comes within 2 tiles of the other twin (along both axes, `collideReach`) in the next second (`collideWindow`, tracked as `magnetMoved`) crashes into it. The crash deals 15% of a life in every phase (`collideShare`, about 248) split to even out their HP (`collisionSplit`): the healthier twin takes damage first until they match, then the rest is split equally. Both are stunned for 5 seconds (`collideStunTicks`) and you heal 15 (`collideHeal`), in a 1.5-second burst: a screen-wide coral/blue tint and overlapping light gradients, five paired open magnetic pressure fronts with pale cores and broad translucent wakes, 88 angular magnetic shards and 44 star-like glints. The full collision fills every camera view, even from another room; an offscreen origin projects to the nearest screen edge in its direction. Layered magnetic corner contacts frame the release; a 40-tick shake at strength 16 and the sci-fi explosion layered with the bomb explosion. Since a twin killed during the stun cannot start reviving until it ends, a collision is the safest way to finish them together.
- **Exposed:** from the crash, the pair is exposed for 12 seconds (the 5-second stun plus 7, `exposeTicks`): no damage cut, and your hits and your F copy's even out the twins' HP (each twin takes 2 × its share of their combined HP, kept within 0.5–1.5×, `exposedSkew`, so the healthier one takes more and the weaker less while the pair as a whole takes full damage; a twin whose partner is dead or down takes 100% instead, `exposedAloneDamage`; meteors stay at full), no bond line (each twin flickers in a white frame), an EXPOSED bar in place of TELEPORT, and no jumps until it ends. Line them up by luring both into your room, then push one into the other or pull one through the other.

## Twin moves

Every twin attack on this map, ordinary or new, deals 85% of its listed damage (`twinDamage`, applied through `twinBerserk`; values below are before it). On this map the twins add three moves to their ordinary attacks (`BINARY.dash`, `starfall`, `beam`; `updateTwinMoves`). A stun or a Pole Shift shove cancels a move in progress; the passive field never does (it can move Dawn while she marks her lane, and she then dashes from where she stands along the same direction, but it leaves her alone mid-dash); berserk shortens their cooldowns and raises the damage of the dash and starfall.

- **Comet Dash (Dawn):** every 7 seconds, when you are 3–7 tiles away and lined up with her (same row or column, one tile of slack), she marks the lane ahead (up to 7 tiles, stopping at walls) for 0.6 seconds, then dashes along it at half a tile per tick. Touching her deals 14 and shoves you 2 tiles; a placed Ruby she passes takes 10. Her ordinary AI pauses while she marks and dashes (`binaryTwinBusy`). Look: coral tiles with chevrons along the lane, flashing white just before she goes, and coral afterimages behind her. Sounds: `charging.wav` as she marks, `slash.mp3` as she goes.
- **Starfall (Dusk):** every 9 seconds when you are within 10 tiles, three 3×3 areas are marked around you (one on your tile, the others up to 3 tiles away, kept apart). After 1.1 seconds each bursts: 12 to you once per volley plus a 0.5-second stun (`stunTicks`, the comet stun), 8 to a placed Ruby in an area. Look: a faint blue square with a closing outline (white at the end) and a falling pale star block, then a white flash, an expanding blue square and four sparks. Sounds: `falling-thing.wav` on marking, `snowball-hit.mp3` per burst.
- **Twin Beam (both):** every 12 seconds, when both twins are in the same chamber at least 3 tiles apart (and not exposed), a dashed coral-to-blue link tracks between them for 1 second, tightening and brightening, then locks and fires for 0.4 seconds: 18 to you within 0.6 tiles of the line, 12 to a placed Ruby. Look: straight stroked coral, blue and white bands. Sounds: `charging.wav` as it links, `laserBeam.mp3` as it fires.

## Pole Shift (C)

- You are north (coral) or south (blue); Pole Shift, the primal skill (C), swaps it (2-second cooldown, `poleCooldown`). The swap plays the Neptune's Tide cast sound (`celestial/neptune-tide.wav`); the shared primal-skill ready recording plays when it recharges. A square frame in your pole's colour surrounds you and flares on a swap.
- **Your Binary Star twin (F)** always holds the opposite pole to yours (`cloneMagnet`), shown by its pole frame, and its field reaches 3 tiles (`cloneRange`; yours reaches 4). A faint dashed square in its pole's colour marks that reach, and the reach is exactly that square: every field (yours and your twin's) reaches enemies within its range in tiles along both axes, corners included (`fieldReaches`). It is never pushed or pulled and has no force on you, but every pulse it pushes or pulls the twins (and Lodestones) in its reach two tiles (`fieldTiles`; a pull stops 1.5 tiles short). **Your field takes priority:** a twin inside your reach answers only to your field, every pulse and on a swap, even when your pull is already holding it close; your twin's field acts only on twins outside your reach. Each Pole Shift flips both of you: what you were pulling, your twin now pushes. With the twins between you and your twin, a swap throws both toward the middle.
- **Surge:** for 0.75 seconds after a swap (`surgeTicks`), each twin (or Lodestone) in your field gets one strong shove from you; one outside your reach but in your twin's gets it from your twin: up to 3 tiles away for a push (`surgeShove`), or toward the source for a pull, stopping just short of it. Each jolt, push or pull alike, also deals 2% of a twin's life (`surgeTwinShare`, 30 HP, ignoring the bond) or 2 to a Lodestone (`surgeDamage`), and heals you and Ruby 10 each (`surgeHeal`). It applies once per swap and at most every 2 seconds per enemy (`surgeTwinCooldown`). A stepped bolt of 8px blocks in the source's pole colour runs from the source (you or your twin) to the enemy, a square burst flashes on it and `celestial/neptune-electric.wav` plays. An enemy shoved into a wall is stunned for 1.5 seconds (`slamStunTicks`), without damage. A twin also gets that stun when any field pulse (yours or your twin's) pushes or pulls it into a wall, or finds it already pressed against one; after a wall stun it cannot be wall-stunned again for 3 seconds from the stun's start (`wallStunCooldown`, `binaryWallStun`), so a field cannot pin it forever.
- On this map your F copy hits as hard as you: its lasers, bullets, waves and bomb blasts deal full damage (60% elsewhere).
- Phase 1 has no room magnets. From phase 2 (`magnetFromPhase`) each room holds one magnet (`magnetsPerRoom`); ALPHA and GAMMA share one pole and BETA and DELTA the other, chosen at random. Magnets sit on open floor at least 6 tiles (along both axes) from their room's teleport pad (`magnetPadGap`), beyond their reach of the pad and the tiles where you can use it. Every 25 seconds (`magnetMoveTicks`) and at each new phase they move to new spots; for the last 1.5 seconds they blink. Dawn is north and Dusk is south.
- **The twins never move you; you move them.** Every 16 ticks (`magnetPulse`) your field (4 tiles) moves each twin in reach two tiles (`fieldTiles`): away if it matches your pole, toward you if it is opposite, stopping 1.5 tiles short, exactly like your F twin's field. Separately, every room magnet and Lodestone within 4 tiles of you adds a force on you: matching poles push you away, opposite poles pull you in (stopping 1.5 tiles away). Nearer sources pull harder; the net force moves you one tile through the shared knockback, or two tiles for a push from within about 2 tiles (`magnetStrong`), so walls, carrying Ruby and Ursa Major apply. There is no knockback shake.
- Chevrons show each field in range: around a twin in your pole's colour (outward when you push it, inward when you pull it), and around a magnet or Lodestone in its own colour for its force on you. Magnets are blocky horseshoes in their pole colour; the minimap shows them as small U marks.

## Lodestones

- Binary Star's own alien (`lodestone`), from phase 1 (`lodestoneFromPhase`): 30% of regular spawns (`lodestoneChance`), at most three alive (`lodestoneMax`). Base 10 HP, a slow (0.8) chaser whose melee deals 8 to you / 5 to Ruby every 55 ticks, ordinary kill rewards.
- **Magnet Snap:** when you are 2–4 tiles away in sight, it locks the lane toward you (its main axis) and warns for 0.6 seconds (`LODESTONE_SNAP` in constants.ts): the Charger's lane squares in its pole colour over 4 tiles, blinking white at the end, with closing brackets on its body and the Charger's lock sound. Then it lunges up to 4 tiles at 6.5 px/tick with a two-block trail; reaching you deals 10 and knocks you back 1 tile (6 to a placed Ruby), with the Charger impact sound. It rests 0.67 seconds after a snap and snaps at most every 2.5 seconds. The passive field (yours or your F twin's) leaves it alone during the warning and the lunge; a swap shove still cancels it.
- Each spawns with a random pole and is a magnet like the twins: within 4 tiles it pushes you if it matches your pole and pulls you otherwise, and the swap surge jolts it like a twin (a shove and 2 damage).
- **Burst on a twin:** a Lodestone that any magnetic force (your field, your twin's, or a swap) drives within 1 tile of a twin in the next second bursts on it, and so does a twin driven within 1 tile of a Lodestone (`lodestoneHitReach`, tracked as `magnetMoved`). The twin takes 8% of its life (`lodestoneHitShare`, 132 HP, ignoring the bond) and a 1-second stun (`lodestoneHitStun`); the Lodestone shatters with ordinary kill rewards. It shows a smaller version of the collision burst without the screen flash, a light shake and the sci-fi explosion. The swap jolt deals only 2 to a Lodestone, so it survives to be used.
- Look: a squat block with a glowing rim in its pole's colour, a dark inset, a dark visor with two pale eyes and a horseshoe magnet for horns, whose tips spark now and then. It uses the shared regular-alien HP bar.

## Pulsar

- Binary Star alien (`pulsar`) from phase 1 (`BINARY.pulsar`): 25% of regular spawns, at most two alive, 12 base HP, a slow melee chaser (6 to you / 4 to Ruby) with a random pole. Alive, it is not magnetic.
- **Death field:** when it dies (`pulsarDied`), its field takes hold on its tile for 2.5 seconds (`fieldTicks`). Every 12 ticks, while you are within 5 tiles (square reach), it moves you 2 tiles: away if it matches your pole, toward its core if it is opposite, stopping a tile short. Then it explodes within 3 tiles: 28 to you, 16 to a placed Ruby, 20 to regular aliens and 3% of a twin's life. Swapping to its colour throws you clear.
- Look: alive, an ordinary square alien in its pole's colour: a glowing rim, a dark inset, two pale eyes over a dark reactor window whose coloured core swells with its beat, and a short antenna whose square tip blinks white on the beat. The field is a square of its reach in that colour with a marching dashed edge, a blast square closing in from the reach toward the 3-tile blast area, a pulsing core and push/pull chevrons, all brightening and flashing white just before it blows. The explosion reuses the crimson trap blast with a short shake and the bomb explosion sound.

## Grounder

- Binary Star alien (`grounder`) from phase 2 (`BINARY.grounder`): one arrives every 20 seconds on its own timer (not part of regular spawns; No Mobs stops it), at most two alive, at least 6 tiles from you. 30 HP, movement speed 1.5, never attacks.
- It walks to the nearest living twin and stays within 2 tiles of it (`updateGrounder`). Any twin within 3 tiles of a living Grounder (square reach) is **grounded** (`grounded`): your field and your twin's field do not move it, a swap does not jolt it, and it cannot take part in a collision. Lodestone bursts still work on it.
- Killing it stuns the twin it was escorting for 1 second (`killStun`).
- Look: a heavy squat slate block with a grey rim, dark inset, two dim eyes and an anchor spike driven into the floor with a pulse running down it. A faint dashed grey square marks its reach; a grounded twin wears a grey frame with four heavy corner blocks.

## Phase 3

- **Pole Reversal** (`BINARY.reversal`, `updatePoleReversal`): each twin flips its own pole on its own timer, every 22–26 seconds (staggered so they rarely flip together), stored on the twin as `pole` (`twinPole`, `twinReversed`). For the last 1.5 seconds its aura blinks between both colours. Every twin in phase 3 wears a pulsing square aura in its current pole colour, and a reversed twin keeps its own body colours; only its aura and its eye (the visor, frame and pale core, in the other twin's shades) show its current pole. A new life starts on its own pole.
- **Charging tiles** (`BINARY.charge`, `updateChargeTiles`): one per room, at least 3 tiles from its teleport pad. A twin below 80% of its life (after a 5s grace) walks to the tile in its room and stands on it; after a 1-second set-up with no healing (`setupTicks`) it heals 1.2% of its life a second (18 HP) until full; it does not attack while seeking or charging (`binaryTwinBusy`). Any push, pull or knockback that moves it while charging ends the charge, and it then waits 16 seconds before seeking again (`failCooldown`; 8 seconds after a full charge, `cooldown`); a jump also ends it. Look (`drawChargeTile`, shared with the rule sheet): a dark recessed plate with coral top/left and blue bottom/right edges holding a pale stepped battery with four cells lit coral to blue. Idle, one cell glows dimly; while a twin charges, the cells fill one after another and mint healing crosses rise. White dots on the minimap.

## HUD and controls

- The guardian HUD shows ASTRAL TWINS · PHASE n over two rows, DAWN and DUSK, each with three life segments (phase 3 on the left). Spent lives are empty; a downed twin's segment drains as its revival timer. Under them, a TELEPORT countdown bar (the same row layout as other maps' bars) drains toward the twins' next opposite-room jump and turns white for the 1.5 second warning.
- The C icon is a blocky magnet in your pole's colour with a chip of the other pole.
- God controls: Jump and Collide sit side by side below the phase buttons. Phase 1/2/3 (summon the pair if needed and start that life at full HP), Jump (start the jump warning now) and Collide (`godCollideTwins`: move Dusk beside Dawn and crash them at once, even if grounded or already exposed; only while both are standing).
